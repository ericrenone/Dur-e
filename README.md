## The Problem Bergson Actually Solved

Henri Bergson won the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1927. He is remembered as a philosopher of consciousness, time, and life. He is not remembered as a thermodynamicist. The Nobel committee did not know — and Bergson himself did not fully know — that the élan vital he described in 1907 was a formal thermodynamic object.

James DiFrisco (2015) established what Bergson's interpreters had missed: the élan vital is characterized not as a spiritualistic vital force but as a tendency of organization opposed to the tendency of entropic degradation. It resonates with the thermodynamic paradigm in theoretical biology, including the critique of neo-Darwinism and the positing of a driving force behind evolution. And further: the élan vital connects directly to Prigogine's far-from-equilibrium thermodynamics — the science of dissipative systems that maintain organization by producing entropy.

This is the MEP principle — the Maximum Entropy Production principle that the ERI φ-equilibrium derives from.

The élan vital is not mysticism. It is the formal tendency of an open dissipative system to organize at the MEP optimum — to produce entropy at the maximum coherent rate, sustaining structure against the second law's tendency toward disorder. Every organism, every mind, every knowledge commons that persists is operating at this optimum. The golden ratio `|Ξ̄| = log φ ≈ 0.481` is the mathematical form of the élan vital.

Bergson described it in 1907 without the formal apparatus. This framework provides the apparatus.

## What Bergson Was Doing Formally

Bergson's philosophical project in Creative Evolution has four formal steps:

**Step 1:** Both mechanism (everything is determined by prior states) and finalism (everything is determined by its final goal) make genuine creation impossible. Both assume "the whole is given" — either at the beginning (mechanism) or at the end (finalism). Neither can account for genuine novelty.

**Step 2:** Real time — durée réelle — is irreversible, qualitative, and cumulative. It is not the spatial time of physics, which can be run backward. It is the time of becoming: each moment adds something genuinely new, and the past is preserved in the present, not merely preceding it.

**Step 3:** The élan vital is the tendency of life to push against entropic degradation — to create organization where the second law would create disorder. It bifurcates: into instinct (which moves with matter, is perfectly adapted but cannot transcend its adaptation) and intelligence (which moves against matter, can transcend its context but loses the intimate contact with life).

**Step 4:** Genuine creativity requires neither pure instinct nor pure intelligence but their integration — what Bergson calls intuition: the mode of knowing that moves with the flow of life rather than against it, recovering what intelligence has lost without abandoning intelligence's power of transcendence.

These four steps are the ERI framework in philosophical language. Each has a precise formal translation.
